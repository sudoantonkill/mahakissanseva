# Comprehensive Guide to Sugarcane Cultivation in Maharashtra

## 1. Introduction and Climate Requirements
Sugarcane is one of the most important cash crops in Maharashtra. The state is a leading producer of sugar in India. 
- **Climate:** Sugarcane requires a hot and humid climate. Temperature between 28°C to 36°C is ideal for growth. High humidity (above 70%) favors vegetative growth, while dry weather during ripening improves sugar recovery.
- **Soil:** Deep, well-drained, fertile loamy to clayey soils with good water-holding capacity are ideal. The soil pH should be between 6.5 and 8.0. Saline and alkaline soils should be avoided or reclaimed using gypsum before planting.

## 2. Popular Varieties in Maharashtra
- **Co 86032 (Nira):** A widely adapted, high-yielding, and high-sugar variety. Known for its good ratoonability and tolerance to moisture stress. It is a mid-late maturing variety.
- **CoM 0265 (Phule 265):** Developed by MPKV, Rahuri. Highly salt-tolerant and high-yielding. Suitable for saline and sodic soils of Maharashtra.
- **Co 92005:** Early maturing, high sugar variety.
- **VSI 434:** A high-yielding variety suitable for various zones in Maharashtra.

## 3. Planting Seasons and Seed Rate
Maharashtra has three main planting seasons for sugarcane:
- **Adsali:** Planted in July-August. Duration is 16-18 months. Yield is highest.
- **Pre-seasonal:** Planted in October-November. Duration is 14-15 months.
- **Suru (Eksali):** Planted in January-February. Duration is 12-14 months.
- **Seed Rate:** Generally, 25,000 to 30,000 two-budded setts or 10,000 to 12,000 three-budded setts per acre are required. Sett treatment with Carbendazim (0.1%) and Malathion (0.1%) for 15 minutes is crucial to prevent seed-borne diseases and scale insects.

## 4. Fertilizer Management
Sugarcane is a heavy feeder. Fertilizer application should be based on soil testing.
- **Organic:** 10-15 tonnes of FYM (Farm Yard Manure) or compost per acre before planting.
- **Chemical (NPK for Suru crop per hectare):** 250 kg Nitrogen, 115 kg Phosphorus, and 115 kg Potassium.
- Nitrogen should be applied in split doses (at planting, tillering, and grand growth stages).
- **Micronutrients:** Iron and Zinc deficiencies are common in Maharashtra soils. Foliar spray of Ferrous Sulphate (0.5%) and Zinc Sulphate (0.5%) is recommended.

## 5. Water Management (Irrigation)
Sugarcane requires huge amounts of water (1500-2500 mm). 
- **Drip Irrigation:** Highly recommended in Maharashtra to save water (up to 40-50% savings) and increase yield. Fertigation (applying fertilizer through drip) increases nutrient use efficiency.
- **Critical Stages for Irrigation:** Germination (1-35 days), Tillering (36-100 days), Grand Growth (101-270 days), and Ripening (271 days to harvest). Water stress during the grand growth stage severely impacts yield.

## 6. Major Diseases and Management
- **Red Rot (Colletotrichum falcatum):** The most devastating disease. Symptoms include yellowing of leaves, withering, and a sour smell from split canes. Red tissues with white crossbands are seen inside the stalk. 
  - *Management:* Use healthy seed setts, disease-resistant varieties (like Co 86032), and practice crop rotation.
- **Smut (Sporisorium scitamineum):** Characterized by a black, whip-like structure emerging from the growing point.
  - *Management:* Rogue out and destroy infected clumps. Sett treatment with fungicides.
- **Wilt:** Wilting of the crown leaves. Often occurs in association with root borers or nematodes.
  - *Management:* Avoid moisture stress, control borers, and use healthy setts.

## 7. Major Pests and Management
- **Early Shoot Borer (Chilo infuscatellus):** Attacks during the early stages (1-3 months). Causes "dead heart" which can be easily pulled out.
  - *Management:* Trash mulching, early planting, and release of Trichogramma chilonis egg parasitoids. Chemical: Soil application of Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% GR.
- **Internode Borer:** Attacks during the grand growth phase. Holes on internodes with frass.
  - *Management:* Release of Trichogramma chilonis. Detrashing (removal of dry leaves) helps.
- **Whitefly (Aleurolobus barodensis):** Sucks sap from leaves, causing yellowing and stunted growth. Sooty mold develops on honeydew.
  - *Management:* Avoid excess nitrogen. Maintain proper drainage. Spray Imidacloprid if infestation is severe.
- **White Grub:** A major problem in some parts of Maharashtra (like Kolhapur region). Grubs feed on roots causing drying of clumps.
  - *Management:* Deep summer ploughing. Application of Metarhizium anisopliae (fungus) or entomopathogenic nematodes.

## 8. Weed Management
Weed competition is critical in the first 90-120 days.
- Cultural: 2-3 hoeings and hand weedings. Trash mulching in the inter-row spaces suppresses weeds.
- Chemical: Pre-emergence application of Atrazine (2 kg/ha) or Metribuzin. Post-emergence application of 2,4-D (for broad-leaved weeds).

## 9. Harvesting
Harvesting should be done at peak maturity when sugar content is highest (Brix reading of 18-20%). The crop should be cut close to the ground. Late harvesting reduces sugar recovery and weight.
